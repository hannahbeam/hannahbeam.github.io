Where Syracuse’s Public Art Lives

Downtown is bursting with murals, while other neighborhoods see far fewer installations

The data shows that murals make up the majority of public art installations in Syracuse, with the Downtown TNT Area containing more than twice as many pieces as any other neighborhood. Downtown’s total is largely composed of large murals painted on building walls, which aligns with the city’s focus on revitalizing visible public spaces and attracting visitors. The Eastside and Westside also feature several murals, but they include a slightly wider mix of sculptures and monuments. In contrast, the Northside, Southside, and Eastwood have only a few recorded artworks, showing how artistic investment varies across the city. Sculptures and monuments are most often found near the University area and Franklin Square, where older and more permanent installations remain part of the landscape. The dataset also suggests that murals are the most frequently added form of art in recent years, while other types have stayed relatively constant. Overall, the visualization demonstrates that public art in Syracuse is concentrated in central and high-traffic areas rather than being evenly distributed across neighborhoods. This pattern raises important questions about how public art reflects access, representation, and the priorities that shape cultural investment in the city.
